# Why Play Go?

A few simple rules. Endless possibilities.

Go is one of the world's oldest games, played in China for more than 2,500 years. Two players take turns placing black and white stones on a board, each trying to surround more territory than the other.

You can learn the rules in five minutes. Mastering them can take a lifetime. The good news is that the fun starts long before mastery.

## You learn to see

At first, a stone is just a stone. Soon you start to notice which groups are strong, which are in danger, and where the opportunities are. Every game, you see a little more.

## You don't have to win every fight

Give up a corner to gain a side. Sacrifice a few stones to build something bigger. Go rewards the big picture, not every small battle.

## Every move matters

Go asks you to slow down, look carefully and choose well. Even a lost game teaches you something.

## The tricky part: the ending

Many beginners find the end of a game confusing, and that's normal. The board fills up, and it's hard to tell who owns what or whether any useful moves are left.

A game ends when both players pass in turn, or when one player resigns, often before the score has been counted. Recognising that moment is a skill you'll build.

## That's where Go Coach helps

When the computer passes, it is telling you nothing is left worth playing. In Coach mode, the Territory map shows who controls each area, so you can learn to see it for yourself.

You don't play Go just to win. You play to think, to discover and to enjoy getting better.
