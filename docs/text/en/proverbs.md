# Go sayings

One saying sums up the evidence when the coach explains why one move beats another ("why A beats B").
"principle" and "proverb" are the labels; "sun" and "rules" are the sources. In each ".link", A is the better move and B the weaker one.
Some situations also have words from Go masters and teachers (".master", ".master2" and so on): the coach uses either the saying or one of these, at random. ".label" is optional and replaces "Go master" (for a stratagem, for example).
A master's words that have not been checked against a book or interview are marked "attributed to" ("attributed"); only change that once the source is confirmed.
A master's words that have been checked carry their source in ".master.source" instead, such as "(Edward Lasker, Go and Go-Moku)".

How to edit: change the words under a "## name" heading, but leave the heading itself as it is.
Words in curly brackets, such as {point}, are filled in by the app: keep them.

## sacrifice.text
Sacrifice stones to gain the initiative.

## sacrifice.link
A gives up a stone, but what it gains elsewhere is worth more.

## rescue.text
Don’t go fishing while your house is on fire.

## rescue.link
Saving your own group comes before taking points elsewhere: B leaves it to die.

## kill.text
In war, the way is to avoid what is strong and to strike at what is weak.

## kill.link
The weak group is the target: A catches it, B lets it get away.

## lost.text
Enter the opponent’s sphere gently.

## lost.link
A stone played too deep into the opponent’s area has no room to live, so B only feeds the opponent.

## punished.text
Be careful; do not move rashly.

## punished.link
B leaves a cutting point the opponent can use straight away.

## whole.text
The best thing of all is to take the enemy’s country whole and intact; to shatter and destroy it is not so good.

## whole.link
A gains more by building than B gains by attacking.

## big.text
Give up the small to take the large.

## big.link
Both moves have a point, but the area A claims is worth more.

## whole.master.text
Go is harmony.

## whole.master.name
Go Seigen

## whole.master.link
A builds in balance with the whole board; B attacks for its own sake and gains less.

## lost.master.text
Match strength with strength, lightness with lightness.

## lost.master.name
Cho Chikun

## lost.master.link
Against the opponent’s strength, play lightly: B plunges in heavily where it cannot live.

## big.master.text
The Go board is so much larger than the Chess board that a defeat on one wing of the battle-field can often be compensated by victory on another.

## big.master.name
Edward Lasker

## big.master.source
(Edward Lasker, Go and Go-Moku, 1960, page 100)

## big.master.link
Think of the whole battlefield: the ground A claims is worth more than what B fights for.

## whole.master2.text
Go is often called the ‘art of harmony.’ As it is a contest for victory between two players, a self-centered way of thinking often leads to self ruin.

## whole.master2.source
(The Nihon Ki-in, Go: The World’s Most Fascinating Game, 1973)

## whole.master2.link
A builds without overreaching; B thinks only of its own attack, and gains less.

## kill.master.text
In go the best moves tend to be those which serve more than one purpose, and the fastest way to build territory is to do so while attacking.

## kill.master.link
A attacks the weak group and gains ground at the same time; B lets it go.
