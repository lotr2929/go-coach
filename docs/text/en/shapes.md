# Shapes

The Shapes tab and the coach's answers about shapes ("what is a ponnuki?"). For each shape: its name, the name with "a" or "an" (".a"),
a short explanation (".text") and the fuller one given when you tap it or ask about it (".more"). The rest are the pieces of the lines in the chat.

How to edit: change the words under a "## name" heading, but leave the heading itself as it is.
Words in curly brackets, such as {point}, are filled in by the app: keep them.

## ponnuki.name
Ponnuki

## ponnuki.a
a ponnuki

## ponnuki.text
The diamond left after capturing one stone. Very strong and influential: a proverb says a ponnuki is worth 30 points.

## ponnuki.more
A ponnuki is the diamond of four stones left after you capture a single stone: one stone above, below, left and right of the empty point. It has no cutting points, plenty of liberties and faces every direction, so its strength radiates across the board. The proverb says a ponnuki is worth 30 points: capturing a stone this way, especially early in the game, is usually a big gain even though only one stone was taken.

## tiger.name
Tiger’s mouth

## tiger.a
a tiger’s mouth

## tiger.text
Three stones around an empty point. A stone played inside can be captured at once, so the shape is safely connected and flexible.

## tiger.more
A tiger’s mouth (also called a hanging connection) is three stones around one empty point, like an open mouth. If the opponent plays inside the mouth, that stone has only one liberty and you capture it at once. So the stones are as good as connected without spending a move to fill the point, and the shape stays light and flexible. Watch out for ko: if the opponent can play inside as a ko threat or sacrifice, the mouth can become a weakness.

## bamboo.name
Bamboo joint

## bamboo.a
a bamboo joint

## bamboo.text
Two pairs of stones with a gap between them. If the opponent pushes into one gap, you fill the other, so it can never be cut.

## bamboo.more
A bamboo joint is two pairs of side-by-side stones with a gap between the pairs, like a joint in a bamboo stem. There are two ways through the gap: if the opponent pushes into one, you fill the other. So the shape can never be cut, and it uses no more stones than it needs. It is one of the strongest ways to connect.

## diagonal.name
Diagonal

## diagonal.a
a diagonal

## diagonal.text
Two stones touching at a corner. If the opponent plays one connecting point you play the other, so they stay connected.

## diagonal.more
A diagonal (kosumi in Japanese) is two stones touching corner to corner. There are two empty points that would join them. If the opponent plays on one, you play the other, so the stones cannot be cut. It is slow but very solid. If the opponent already holds one of the two points, the other one becomes a cutting point.

## jump.name
One-point jump

## jump.a
an one-point jump

## jump.text
Stones one space apart. Quick, and hard to cut directly: "the one-point jump is never bad".

## jump.more
A one-point jump (ikken tobi) is two stones on the same line with one empty point between them. It moves quickly into open space, and the opponent cannot cut it directly: a stone pushed into the gap can be blocked from either side. A proverb says "the one-point jump is never bad".

## knight.name
Knight’s move

## knight.a
a knight’s move

## knight.text
Light and fast, but the opponent can push through the middle and try to cut it.

## knight.more
A knight’s move (keima) is two stones placed like a knight in chess: one step one way and two steps the other. It is a fast, light way to approach or run, but it is thinner than a one-point jump: the opponent can push into one of the two middle points and try to cut through at the other.

## triangle.name
Empty triangle

## triangle.a
an empty triangle

## triangle.text
Three stones in an L with the fourth point empty. Three stones doing the work of two, with fewer liberties: usually bad shape.

## triangle.more
An empty triangle is three of your stones in an L, with the fourth corner of the little square empty. The three stones have fewer liberties than three stones spread out and cover less ground, so you have used three stones to do the work of two. It is the most famous bad shape. Sometimes it is the only way to escape or connect, so it is not always wrong, but look for a better move first.

## dumpling.name
Dumpling

## dumpling.a
a dumpling

## dumpling.text
Four stones in a solid block. Very slow: the stones are bunched up instead of claiming space.

## dumpling.more
A dumpling is four of your stones in a solid 2×2 block. They are safe but very slow: four stones that claim almost no space and have only eight liberties between them. It usually happens when you keep answering the opponent’s pushes. Aim for shapes that cover more ground with the same stones.

## cut.name
Cutting point

## cut.a
a cutting point

## cut.text
A weak spot: the opponent already touches this diagonal, so a stone on the marked point would cut the two stones apart.

## cut.more
A cutting point is an empty point where the opponent could separate two of your groups. Here your two stones touch only at a corner, and the opponent already holds one of the two points between them, so a stone on the other point cuts them apart. Either connect there, or make sure each part can look after itself.

## crosscut.name
Crosscut

## crosscut.a
a crosscut

## crosscut.text
Both sides cut each other. A fighting shape: the side with stronger stones nearby usually comes out ahead.

## crosscut.more
A crosscut is two of your stones and two of the opponent’s crossing diagonally in a square, so each side cuts the other. It starts a fight. The proverb "when in doubt, extend from a crosscut" means: add a stone straight out from one of your cut stones to give it more liberties.

## eye.name
Eye

## eye.a
an eye

## eye.text
An empty point surrounded by one colour. A group with two real eyes can never be captured.

## eye.more
An eye is an empty point completely surrounded by one colour. The opponent may play there only if it captures. A group with two separate real eyes can never be captured, because the opponent would have to fill both at once. Making two eyes is the key to life and death.

## falseeye.name
False eye

## falseeye.a
a false eye

## falseeye.text
It looks like an eye, but the opponent holds the corners, so it can be filled. It does not count towards the two eyes a group needs.

## falseeye.more
A false eye looks like an eye, but the opponent holds enough of its diagonal corners (two in the middle of the board, one on the edge) that the stones around it can be put in atari and captured. When that happens the "eye" disappears. A group needs two real eyes; false eyes do not count.

## shape-term
Shape means how your stones are arranged. Good shape (tiger’s mouth, bamboo joint, ponnuki) uses few stones to be strong and connected; bad shape (empty triangle, dumpling) wastes stones. Turn on the Shapes tab under the board and I will mark the shapes on the board; tap a marked stone and I will explain its shape.
