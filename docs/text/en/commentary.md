# Commentary on moves

What the coach says about a move: in Coach mode, for hints, for "why?", for a review and for "explain the position".
The sentences are put together from the pieces below. "idea." lines are the one-sentence idea behind each kind of move.
{mine}, {theirs} are words such as "your" or "Black’s"; {who}, {them} are "you", "Black" or "White"; {Mine} and {Theirs} are the same with a capital letter.

How to edit: change the words under a "## name" heading, but leave the heading itself as it is.
Words in curly brackets, such as {point}, are filled in by the app: keep them.

## idea.capture
Idea: a captured stone is gone for good. Before each move, ask: can I capture something safely?

## idea.rescue
Idea: check for atari before every move. A group with one liberty can vanish in a single move.

## idea.atari
Idea: atari is a threat, not a capture. It works best when the group cannot escape, or when the reply it forces helps you.

## idea.connect
Idea: connected stones share their liberties. Stones left apart can be attacked one at a time.

## idea.cut
Idea: cutting turns one strong group into two weak ones, and weak groups have to spend moves defending.

## idea.territory
Idea: your score is your stones plus the empty points you surround, so claiming big empty areas early is efficient.

## idea.approach
Idea: a stone placed near the opponent’s stone limits how much space that stone can claim.

## idea.build
Idea: stones that support each other make a position strong, and a strong position makes territory easier to hold.

## idea.boundary
Idea: where two sides meet, each move decides how far each side’s area reaches. Moves on the boundary are often the biggest on the board.

## idea.line
Idea: the third line is the "line of territory" (safe and good for points) and the fourth is the "line of influence" (more power towards the centre).

## checklist
Five questions to ask before every move:
1. Is any group in atari, mine or my opponent’s?
2. Can I capture something, or must I rescue something?
3. Are my groups connected, or can the opponent cut between them?
4. Where is the biggest empty area, and can I claim it?
5. Will my new stone have enough liberties to be safe?
Answer them in that order: danger first, then opportunity, then territory.
