# Go Coach: direction and decisions

A summary of Boon's decisions and the reasoning behind them, so any session (phone, cloud or laptop) can pick up where the last one stopped. The code changes themselves are in `docs/phone-notes/`. Update this file when a decision changes.

Last updated: 27 November 2026.

## Hosting and business (Boon, 26 Nov 2026)
- **Moving from Vercel to Cloudflare Pages** (free plan allows commercial use; Vercel's free plan does not). Free address on pages.dev (e.g. go-coach.pages.dev); no domain name until subscribers reach the hundreds. Cloudflare publishes the repository as it is (no build step; the repository is public anyway); `_headers` does what `vercel.json` did. Live at https://go-coach.pages.dev since 26 Nov 2026 (checked: same version as main). Vercel stays until Boon switches it off.
- Boon has checked with Curtin. ABN first (sole trader); payments to PayPal to start (a free PayPal Business account recommended, as subscriptions need one).
- Note: what a device stores (the device key for cloud saves, chat history, settings) belongs to the web address, so it does not carry over from the Vercel address to the Cloudflare one.

## Beta and subscriptions (Boon, 27 Nov 2026)
- **Free beta now, subscriptions in the new year** once we have seen how much people like it. Everything is open during the beta (the free-to-10-kyu limit is not switched on, and the 3-games-a-day limit in Puzzle mode, i.e. Go Coach mode, is off: `BETA` in gcmode.js). The welcome starts with a sentence saying Go Coach is in beta; the word "beta" opens docs/text/en/pages/beta.md (what beta means, keeping progress, feedback by the envelope, what the usage log records, what happens after the beta).
- **How we judge the beta:** players counted by an anonymous device number in the usage log (telemetry.js `start` event, `device` and `signedIn`; only while the log is on), how often they return, and how long they stay (session times).
- **Discussion board, a subscriber extra for the new year (Boon, 28 Nov 2026; idea only, nothing built):** a small discussion board (topics with replies, not a live chat room), inspired by Sensei's Library's "Philosophy and Go". Everyone can read; only subscribers can post, which keeps spam and the number of posts down. A few sections (e.g. Go and philosophy, questions about your games, suggestions for Go Coach). Claude checks new posts once or twice a day on a schedule (posts appear after the check), answers Go questions and sends Boon a short summary; Boon is responsible for what is posted and decides hard cases (complaints, bans). Boon wants it helpful, not just useful: a "This helped me" button (no thumbs-down) ranks replies; the asker can mark a best answer; Claude writes a short summary at the top of each settled topic; labels by topic and level (e.g. "Life and death, 25 kyu"); a "Best of" page that grows into a small guide; short house rules (explain kindly, no mocking beginners), held back in the daily check when broken. Likely built on the envelope messages (messages.js and its Supabase functions).
- **Before charging (December):** real sign-in (Supabase Auth: email code or Google, undecided) so a subscription follows the player across devices; a member flag per account; payments (PayPal or Stripe: undecided, Stripe is usually cheaper per payment in Australia) telling Supabase who has paid; ABN; privacy policy and terms.

## Opening and signing in (Boon, 27 Nov 2026; replaces "one account per device")
- **The app opens fresh every time, as a guest:** a new board and the welcome in the chat, no automatic tour, nothing from earlier visits.
- **A guest's data is wiped at the next open** (account.js): everything stored under a `gocoach.` name, including settings, the game, the chat and its history, grade, streak, lessons done and the cloud-save/messages key. Kept for the device only: the usage-log switch and its anonymous device number.
- **Signing in is typing a name** (no password yet). It brings back that name's data; a new name becomes a new account and keeps what the guest did so far. **A guest's puzzles, lesson ticks, streak and grade are not recorded at all** (Boon, 27 Nov 2026): Puzzle mode works for the visit but records only once signed in. Several names can share a device. Straight after signing in, the game and chat carry on where they were. Signed-in data is put away under `gcacct/<name>/` at the next open. A reload during a signed-in visit stays signed in; closing the app or tab signs out.
- On upgrade, a device with a name already keeps its data under that name; a device with no name starts fresh.

## Product
- **Go Coach first.** Develop it until Boon is satisfied before approaching anyone. World of Go (neural network, subscription) comes later, funded by traction.
- **Go Coach stays local:** rules and random play-outs, no neural network, no network calls except the optional usage logging.
- **Audience and message:** attract competitive people and show them, with evidence, that restraint wins. Go as a war game you win by building, not destroying.

## Look

Boon admires SmartGo's clean design but asked for our own, not a copy. All colours are tokens at the top of style.css. ## The chat (25 Nov 2026)
- **One list, not exchanges (changed 25 Nov 2026, later the same day):** the chat is a single list under the session's date and time, oldest first. Only the newest coach's message shows in full; every earlier item is one line starting with a dash (what you typed, or the first line of a coach's message). There is no "Coach's notes" heading (it opened nothing new); the welcome keeps its "Welcome" heading. A folded coach's "Position" or "Your move" message is not listed at all; tap a folded coach's line to read it all.
- **Sessions:** each starts with a date and time. Only pressing New starts a new session: the board and the chat are cleared and the old session moves into the history. Leaving the app or reopening it always returns you to where you were (Boon, 25 Nov 2026; an earlier one-hour rule was dropped).
- **History:** pull the chat down from the top (or scroll up past the top, or tap "Pull down to see earlier sessions") to see earlier sessions, all folded. The free tier keeps the last 100 sessions on the device; a paid tier (not built yet) would keep them all. If the device's storage runs out, the oldest go first. Sessions from earlier days (by the device's own date and time zone) fold into their day: tap the day for its sessions (listed by time), a session for its lines. Earlier sessions from today show only their date and time. Every heading that opens and closes (day, session) is clickable text, in italics with a thin dotted underline, like the coach's choices.

- **Only the newest coach's message shows in full:** every earlier message folds to its first line, shown as clickable text (italics, thin dotted underline) with a dash in front; tap it to read it all, tap the text again to fold it (Boon chose this over hiding earlier messages behind a count).
- **Requests in your own words (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** if someone asks for anything the app has, in a way that plainly means it, the coach should take them there without Boon having to ask for each phrase. When adding a feature or a page, add the obvious ways of asking for it (coach.js, welcome.js) and a test. Examples: "show me the welcome message" (same as Help), "basic rules" (the step-by-step teacher), "guided game" (the guided first game).
- **Teach me opens the lessons list (Boon, 25 Nov 2026).** The second welcome ("Welcome to Go", after Teach me) was removed; the welcome comes back with Help or by asking for it.
- **Stone counts instead of "Your move" (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** the top of the board shows "● 4  ○ 3" (Black first, both in the counts and the Prisoners on the right) with drawn stones as tall as the letters; the colour you play is underlined (in Play both sides, the side to move; in Set up, the colour being placed, tapped to switch). Words appear there only for scoring, the result and analysis in progress. The counts now show in silent Play mode too. Prisoners use the same drawn stones. Prisoners show as stones lost, no label: "●−2 ○0" (plain 0, never −0); tapping or hovering shows "Prisoners" (Boon, 25 Nov 2026).
- **Lists are tight:** items in any list (the welcome's choices, the lessons menu, the chat's days, sessions and questions) sit close together, with no extra space between lines. Keep this for future lists.

## Saving games (25 Nov 2026)
- **Save button** (between Resign and New): keeps the game in the cloud (Supabase, the shared `dlbs` project, Boon's choice). Pressing it again overwrites the same saved game. **New game box has two tabs:** "New game" (the options) and "Saved games" (every saved game, named by its date and time until Boon renames it; tap to open, Rename, Delete).
- **No real sign-in yet (Boon: "don't worry about that for now").** Until accounts exist each device (now each name signed in on it, see "Opening and signing in") holds a private random key (`gocoach.owner` in localStorage) and the table `gocoach_saves` is reachable only through five functions (`gocoach_save/list/load/rename/delete`) that check the key; row level security with no policies blocks direct access. So saves are private to the device and do not follow Boon to another device or survive clearing the browser's data. When accounts are built (email code, password or Google: undecided), link saves through the table's `user_id` column. Anonymous sign-in is switched off in the project and email sign-in needs confirmation.
- The code is in `cloudsave.js` only; wording in `docs/text/en/save.md`. Open question for Boon: Go Coach's public users would share the `dlbs` project with his private Mobius data; a separate project was recommended before the public release.

## First install

## The coach's voice
- **Realistic, not idealistic or moralistic.** Never "you should be nice"; always "here is what each move costs or gains". Ethics are justified by better outcomes, as in Sun Tzu.
- **Evidence first, saying last.** The "Why X beats Y" message plays both moves out and reports points, which groups live or die and where. One fitting principle or proverb comes at the end, tied back to the position (`proverbs.js`).
- **Quotes must be real and attributed:** Sun Tzu (Giles translation), the Ten Golden Rules of Go, traditional proverbs, and words from Go masters. A master's quote not yet checked against a book or interview is shown as "attributed to" (Boon's choice, 24 Nov 2026): Go Seigen "Go is harmony", Cho Chikun "Match strength with strength, lightness with lightness". Verified: Lee Sedol's 2019 retirement remark about AI (not used yet); Edward Lasker, Go and Go-Moku (Dover 1960), quotes checked against the book (p. 100 in the coach, p. 102 in the tiger's mouth lesson). Checked against books Boon supplied: the Nihon Ki-in (Go: The World's Most Fascinating Game, 1973, "art of harmony"), Kaoru Iwamoto (Go for Beginners, 1972, pp. 104-107), Sakata Eio (in Go on Go, p. 133), Ma Xiaochun's Thirty-Six Stratagems Applied to Go. Go on Go and Yuan Zhou's Master Play do not contain "Go is harmony", so it stays "attributed". To check: Kageyama's "One cannot expect to turn thickness directly into territory" (Lessons in the Fundamentals of Go).
- **Reading the fight (Boon, 27 Nov 2026):** when stones are short of liberties, the analysis explains the position the way Boon wrote it out for one game: weak opponent stones by their liberties, whether your own stones are safe, the best move with the opponent's sensible answers read out (connect, play elsewhere, counter-atari) and what each wins, then the other suggestions (good but smaller, works only if, escapes, does not need it yet) and "Answer: A (E8)". It replaces the percentage list in that case (`reading.js`, wording in `reading.md`). The best move is the one that wins most against the opponent's best answer, so it may not be the letter with the highest percentage. With nothing short of liberties, the usual analysis is shown.
- **War vocabulary is welcome** (terrain, formation, engagement, campaign), with the Go term in brackets for Go players.

## How Go is described (`whatisgo.md`)
- Few rules; everything else emerges from play.
- Four skills: reading the terrain (territory), judging formations (shape), calculating the engagement (reading), commanding the campaign (strategy).
- Winning without destroying: restraint wins in a contest where almost anything goes.

## Shapes tab (Boon, 27 Nov 2026)
- Shapes are drawn light, with borders clear of the stones, so overlapping shapes stay readable. Resting the mouse on a shape's number (or tapping it on a phone) draws that shape strongly, fades the others and pops up what it is; a tap on a number never plays a move.
- The numbers follow the list: Black's shapes (good first, bad last), then White's, then the shapes that could be made next.

## Go Coach grade (Boon, 25 Nov 2026; version 1 built 27 Nov 2026)
- **Lessons folder (Boon, 27 Nov 2026):** lessons are SGF files in `lessons/<grade band>/`, one per file, so several people or Claude accounts can write them without clashing (lessons/README.md has the format; file numbers by writer: Boon's personal account 0001–4999, another account 5000–9999). `make-lessons.js` checks them and builds `lessons/index.json`; `problems.js` reads a lesson and follows the moves; `lessonfiles.js` fetches them from Go Coach's own site and opens them in the usual lesson view, under "Lessons by grade" in the Lessons menu; each counts towards the grade at its own DI. Twelve lessons so far (30 to 19 kyu), seven of them read out by `tools/lessons/tactics.js` (a capture and escape reader that writes each lesson's answer tree and refuses wrong claims; it found two mistakes in the hand-written lessons). Their words are in the SGF files, not docs/text (translation later). Next: many more lessons (checked with KataGo), a daily lesson at the player's grade, a streak.
- **Go Coach mode (Boon, 27 Nov 2026, `gcmode.js`):** tapping the title "Go Coach" (or its logo) turns the daily game on or off; the title becomes a piece of the board (the board's wood and grain) while on. On: today's lesson, picked at the player's grade from the lessons by grade (nearest difficulty, not yet solved, the same all day); solving a lesson in the mode keeps a streak of days (🔥 beside the grade; lapses after a missed day); Next keeps going at the player's grade. The grade with two decimals is the journey (Boon: no separate progress display). Leaving the lessons leaves the mode. On this device until sign-in.
- **The selling point (Boon, 27 Nov 2026): a lesson a day.** Go Coach should feel like a phone game: a large set of progressive lessons the player solves one a day, like a daily crossword, steadily building their grade; that is what keeps players coming back. Needed: many more lessons, ordered by difficulty and placed on the grade scale (built and checked with KataGo, as planned for problems); a daily lesson picked at the player's grade; a streak of days. Lessons and problems are one ladder.
- **Version 1 (27 Nov 2026, `grade.js`):** the grade moves after every game against the computer and every lesson exercise (Boon: "from both games and lessons"), shown at the top beside the name as "18 kyu (17.62)", with a Settings switch "Show my Go Coach grade at the top" (Boon: so others do not see it when he shows the app). Computer levels: 9×9 Easy 30, Medium 20, Hard 8 kyu (measured); 13×13 and 19×19 estimated. A handicap stone is worth 3 grades on 9×9, 2 on 13×13, 1 on 19×19. Only games without help count (Boon, 27 Nov 2026): not with Coach mode, Analyse, hints or undo, nor the guided game, both sides, Set up or games under 10 moves; the chat says so when a game did not count. Lessons count in full (Boon: "lessons must count"), first try only, never after a hint; difficulty 30 kyu (first lesson) to 18 kyu (last). New players start at 25 kyu. Kept on the device until sign-in is built; free-to-10-kyu limit not built yet.
- **Selling point (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** players subscribe to establish and build their Go Coach grade, and can quote it, e.g. when joining a Go club: "my Go Coach grade is 10 kyu". The grade quoted is the whole number only; the decimals are only for watching progress.
- **Goal:** a subscriber builds a grade in Go Coach from the problems they solve, the lessons they do and the games they play, and it becomes fairly accurate over time.
- **Go Coach's own scale, not an official rank.** Shown as "Go Coach grade", in kyu, with a short note that it is Go Coach's own scale (do not suggest it is official or endorsed; Australian consumer law forbids misleading claims).
- **Wording (Boon, 26 Nov 2026):** always "Go Coach grade" (Go Coach's own ranking). The note says we try to keep it in line with international standards but cannot guarantee it.
- **Free to 10 kyu, members above (Boon, 26 Nov 2026):** every player gets a Go Coach grade free, from 30 kyu up to 10 kyu; a grade stronger than 10 kyu needs a subscription. The grade is the main reason to subscribe.
- **Display only the grade, with two decimals** (e.g. 10.63 kyu), so it moves after every game, problem session or lesson, up or down. Boon prefers the excitement of it moving to a steadier one-decimal figure. Kyu counts down as the player improves; after 1.00 kyu comes 1 dan.
- **Display (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** the whole grade with the precise estimate in brackets, e.g. "10 kyu (9.56)". The estimate moves after every game, problem session or lesson; the whole number is the stable, reliable grade. Promotion: the whole number goes up once the estimate has passed the next grade (e.g. 9.00) and stayed there for about 3 games or problem sessions. Demotion: only once the estimate is a clear margin below (e.g. 11.30 for a 10 kyu) and stays there.
- **New game box (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** replaces Easy, Medium and Hard with "Computer plays at your grade: 10 kyu". The grade is underlined (clickable) and the player may change it. If the player has set their own grade, it is shown as "10 (7.25)": the whole number is the player's own setting, the figure in brackets is Go Coach's estimate (here stronger than the setting; "10 (13.64)" would mean Go Coach's estimate is weaker, as kyu numbers fall as players improve). If the player has not set one, the whole number is Go Coach's stable grade, as above. The computer plays at the grade shown as the whole number.
- **Never refuse the player's level (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** the computer always plays at the grade the player sets, whatever Go Coach's own estimate says; the estimate is only shown in brackets, never used to overrule or nag. Above the engine's ceiling (about 6 to 8 kyu on 9×9, lower on larger boards) it still gives a game of that strength by taking handicap stones (or extra komi), each with a measured value, and says so plainly in one short line (e.g. "To play like 3 kyu I'll take 2 stones."). Below Easy's strength it can weaken further or give the player stones. Games played this way still count towards the grade.
- **Publish the method** (Boon's hope): so other Go apps can use the same way of grading and, over time, help build a common, widely accepted ranking. Ways to make it more accurate later: games against real people (e.g. through a partner Go server), checking against association ranks (e.g. the Australian Go Association pilot) and publishing how well they match, and results on larger boards.
- **All three board sizes (Boon's wish, 25 Nov 2026):** one grade fed by 9×9, 13×13 and 19×19 games, each computer level measured against KataGo separately on each size (a level is not the same strength on every board). Go Coach's engine is weaker on larger boards, so 19×19 games may only grade players up to a lower level than 9×9.
- **How it works:** a rating like the European Go Federation's (100 points per grade). Every problem, every lesson exercise and every computer level has a fixed place on the scale; results against them move the rating. New players move faster at first. Games with undo, hints or Coach mode, the guided first game and problems solved after seeing the answer count less or not at all.
- **Problems:** built with KataGo outside the app (answers checked, difficulty set by KataGo's human-style model: the rank that finds the answer about half the time), sorted by topic and linked to the lessons. Simple ones are also checked exhaustively by our own code. KataGo's human model only goes down to 20 kyu, so 30 to 20 kyu difficulties start as estimates, corrected later by players' results. Problems can grade players beyond the engine's ceiling, and work on every board size.
- **Where problems are kept (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** the full set stays on the server; a player downloads only the problems around their grade (about 0.5 KB a problem, so 3,000 is about 1.5 MB in all). Suggested: plain files on Go Coach's own site (Cloudflare Pages), one per grade band, fetched when needed and then kept by the app's offline cache, so problems already downloaded work offline. Fetching the app's own problem files from its own site is allowed (Boon, 25 Nov 2026): the third exception to rule 2, after usage logging and cloud saves. The player's progress stays on the device (later with their account).
- **Problems at any level on request (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** by default the app offers problems around the player's grade, but a player can ask for any level (e.g. "give me 5 kyu problems", "something easier"); that band is downloaded when asked for. The chat should understand the obvious ways of asking. These still count towards the grade at their own difficulty: solving one well above your grade raises it a lot, one well below barely moves it, so the grade stays fair.
- **Release first, extras later (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** Go Coach can go online once the basic structure works: players find their grade and build it through problems and games. Everything below marked "later" is future work, not needed for release.
- **Later: dan problems up to 6 dan** (as GoProblems.com does). Problems can grade beyond the engine's ceiling, so Go Coach's grade can reach dan through problems (this replaces "dan grades belong to World of Go" for problems; games stay limited by the engine). Answers checked with KataGo's normal model and deeper search (several minutes a problem without a graphics card); sources: the 100 famous games and KataGo's games at dan ranks. Shown as provisional ("6 dan?") until enough players have tried them. Build after the kyu problems.
- **Later: ideas from GoProblems.com** (Boon's screenshot, 25 Nov 2026; it grades players from problems only, shown to two decimals): problems of the day at a few levels; timed modes (not counted towards the grade); topic collections with progress; provisional difficulty marked "?". Do not copy their problems; use them only for ideas and as a rough cross-check of difficulty. Go Coach's difference: grading from problems, lessons and games together, with a coach and a computer that plays at your grade.
- **How many problems (25 Nov 2026): none built yet.** Target: about 750 to start (about 30 per grade from 30 kyu to 5 kyu), growing to 2,000 to 3,000 so keen subscribers do not run out. A player's grade needs about 20 to 30 problems near their level for a first estimate (about ±2 grades), 50 for about ±1.5, 100 or more for about ±1. Only the first try at each problem counts. Roughly a minute of computing per problem on a computer without a graphics card, so a few hundred per session.
- **The computer plays at the player's grade, not Easy, Medium or Hard (Boon, 25 Nov 2026; replaces the ladder idea).** The player's grade is built up over time, or the player sets it themselves at the start (e.g. "I'm about 15 kyu"). A self-set grade is only a starting point: it is treated as uncertain and corrected quickly by results. The computer's strength is a setting on the kyu scale, tuned (thinking time and the kind of mistakes made) and checked against KataGo on each board size; games at the player's own grade are the most informative for the rating. Go Coach's engine tops out around 5 to 10 kyu on 9×9 (lower on larger boards); above that the player gives the computer handicap stones. Dan grades belong to World of Go.
- **KataGo is a measuring and building tool only.** It never goes into Go Coach's code (no neural network in the app); only its results (problems, difficulty levels, level settings) do. Its tools live in `tools/katago/` in this repository (Boon, 25 Nov 2026), never loaded by the app or published; cloud sessions on the **go-coach** environment install KataGo automatically (setup script).
- **World of Go** would later give a more accurate, KataGo-based grade on the same scale, carrying over the Go Coach grade.
- **Privacy:** grading on the device needs no permission. Sending players' games away for analysis needs a privacy note, anonymised data and a switch to turn it off; anything personal (e.g. problems from your own games) is opt-in. Lawyer check before charging.
- **Accounts** are needed for the grade to follow a subscriber across devices; until then it stays on one device.

## Game mode: the hook (Boon, 27 Nov 2026)
- **A game is one decisive move, like a chess puzzle** (one step, at most a few): a position cut from the opening, middle or end of a famous game (or a KataGo game), where the player finds the move that mattered. It need not be a kill or a clear win; the point is how one step can be so decisive. After it, a short, interesting explanation of what it was about (the game, the players, why the move mattered).
- **Free players get 3 a day; subscribers have no daily limit.**
- **The player sets the level of the games they want**, above or below their grade; the grade keeps moving fairly (a hard one solved raises it a lot, an easy one barely moves it).
- **It must be addictive:** quick to start, finished in a minute or two, a streak, the grade moving after every game, always "one more".
- **KataGo builds it** (laptop install in tools/katago/bin: v1.18.1 OpenCL, the b10c384 net for fast screening, the b11c768 net to confirm, the human-style model to rank). tools/katago/find-decisive.js finds positions where the move played is also the best and the next best is clearly worse. Claude writes the short stories; they are checked against the kifu's own details (players, event, date), never invented.
- **Speed on the laptop (Intel Arc graphics, OpenCL):** b11c768 about 3 visits a second on 19x19, b10c384 about 64, so the small net screens and the big net confirms.

## Puzzle mode and signing in (Boon, 27 Nov 2026)
- **"Game mode" is renamed Puzzle mode** so it is not confused with a normal game. The title tap turns it on. Puzzles (puzzles/) and lessons (lessons/) are separate lists: each lesson teaches a principle, and several puzzles can be built around the same principle (puzzles are not yet tagged by principle).

- **Busy-board puzzles (Boon, 27 Nov 2026):** "find the group in atari and save it" or "find the stone in atari and capture it" on a busy board from a famous game, to teach reading the board. ## The computer resigns and passes first (Boon, 27 Nov 2026)
- It resigns when clearly lost (playouts give it under 3% and it is well behind, three turns in a row, after half the board's worth of moves), and passes first when nothing is left worth playing (no atari, every empty area surrounded by one colour). Before, it never resigned and passed only after the player. endgame.js; not in the guided first game.

## Engine (search.js)
- Tree search with RAVE over fast play-outs; used by Hard, the coach's hints, the win-chance bar and the explanations.
- Measured: beat the old engine 20–0 (9×9) and 4–0 (13×13); against GNU Go 3.8 on 9×9 at Hard, 9 wins and 7 losses (about 5–10 kyu). Wins are close; losses are large, from misjudging whether a big group lives.
- **Measured against KataGo (25 Nov 2026),** imitating human ranks with its human-style model, 9×9, 151 games (results and tools in `tools/katago/`). Go Coach's computer won: Easy 0/8 against 20 kyu even, 8/30 taking 5 stones; Medium 10/30 against 20 kyu, 8/30 against 15 kyu; Hard 7/8 against 15 kyu, 25/30 against 10 kyu, 4/15 against 5 kyu. Reading: Hard about 7 to 8 kyu (agrees with GNU Go); Medium about 20 kyu or a little weaker; Easy far weaker (around 30 kyu or below, off the bottom of KataGo's scale). Medium did about as well against 15 kyu as against 20 kyu, so KataGo's weakest ranks seem hard to tell apart on 9×9; low grades will need players' own results to settle. Big gaps between levels (Easy to Medium, Medium to Hard): the strength setting needs many steps in between.
- **A stronger engine can wait (Boon, 25 Nov 2026)** until there are enough subscribers; until then players above the engine's ceiling get handicap games. Realistic without a neural network: perhaps 1 to 4 kyu on 9×9. Pattern tables learned from KataGo's games would need Boon's approval first.
- Next engine work, if wanted: life and death in the play-outs, ladders, not collapsing when behind.
- **The coach is always the strongest player (Boon, 27 Nov 2026):** there is no coach level setting; the coach always plays expert. Its analysis (the letters A to E) and its hints use the tree search, like Hard, but with more time (6 s on 9×9, 10 s on larger boards for analysis; 8 s for hints) and on every core of the device at once (`parallel.js`, Web Workers, "root parallel": the counts for each first move are added up). The computer uses one core. Measured in a cloud session at one fifth of the real times: the coach with 2.3 to 4 times the computer's time won 4 of 4; see the phone note of 27 Nov 2026 for the 4-core match.
- **The bar above the board shows the points lead, not a win chance (Boon, 27 Nov 2026):** e.g. "● +8.5", komi included, from play-outs (search.js ownership) averaged with the previous position's estimate. Boon found the old win-chance bar wildly off; on his game (`docs/test-games/2026-11-27-boon-bar.sgf`) it jumped by up to 35 points with whose turn it was and showed 50/50 at the end, while the new one moved smoothly and ended at the real result (Black +8.5).

## How we work
- **Cloud sessions** (phone, or the Desktop app set to cloud) clone GitHub fresh and work through pull requests; Boon says "merge it" to put a change live.
- **Laptop (local) sessions** start with `git pull` on `main` in `C:\_Personal\_myApps\myGoCoach`, then read this file and the newest `docs/phone-notes/`.
- GitHub is the meeting point; avoid changing the same thing on two devices at once.
- **Logo and app icon (Boon, 25 Nov 2026):** two realistic stones, black behind and white in front, overlapping, with shine and a soft shadow, resting on one grid point (a cross) on the app's light grey. It replaces the board picture as the app icon (make-icons.js draws it; docs/logo.svg is the same design). Beside the title (and page titles) the same two stones appear with their shadow on the grid cross, no background or border (logo-mark.svg).
- **Text size and full screen (Boon, 28 Nov 2026):** one reading size (`--text` in style.css) for the chat and the reading pages, with every font drawn at the same letter height (font-size-adjust). There are only two full-screen buttons: one for the board panel (beside the prisoners) and one for the chat; both use the device's own full screen. The board panel's full screen shows what is in it: a reading page fills the screen, otherwise the board. Pages have no button of their own. The full-screen board is a simple board to play on: only Pass, Resign and New (Coach mode needs both panels, so it stays in the usual view), lessons, famous games and puzzles keep their own bars. Full screen only adds a class; nothing is moved, so going back is exact. The modes (Boon, 28 Nov 2026): Board + Coach layout (the usual view) for Play mode, Coach mode and Puzzle mode; Board only layout (the board panel full screen with a board on it) is Game mode. Game mode has no coach, with no exceptions (lessons, famous games, puzzles and the guided first game too): shrink the board to reach the coach. In a game against the computer, Game mode shows a Computer panel (computer.js, header "Computer:") listing the computer's moves, passes and resignation; the player cannot type into it. It sits under the score on its side and beneath undo upright. Lessons, famous games and puzzles in Game mode show only the board and their own bars. The board panel full screen with a reading page shows only the page: no Computer panel.
